How to Choose a Web Designer in 2026

The web design market is flooded with options - from freelancers on Fiverr to large agencies charging tens of thousands. Here's how to find the right fit for your business.

June 2026 · 7 min read · WebMaxPro Blog

Why Choosing the Wrong Designer Is So Costly

A bad web design experience doesn't just cost you money - it costs you time, momentum, and often clients. Many business owners have spent €2,000–€5,000 on a website that was delivered late, built on a bloated platform they can't maintain, and that generates zero enquiries. The frustration is real, and it's extremely common.

The good news: the criteria for choosing a great web designer are straightforward once you know what to look for. The bad news is that most people don't know what to look for - and designers know this. This guide will change that.

1. Look at Their Portfolio Critically

Every web designer has a portfolio. But looking at screenshots isn't enough - you need to actually visit the sites they've built and test them. Open the sites on your phone. Check how fast they load. Click around. Are they actually functional, or just pretty pictures?

Look for:

  • Sites that load in under 3 seconds on mobile
  • Clean, readable typography and consistent spacing
  • Clear calls-to-action on every page
  • Sites that work on small screens, not just desktop
  • Diversity - can they do different styles, or do all their sites look the same?

If the portfolio is all screenshots but no live links, that's a red flag. If all the sites look identical with only the colours changed, the designer likely uses a single template for every client.

2. Ask About Their Technical Process

How a designer builds your site matters enormously for your long-term success. The two main approaches are template-based (WordPress themes, Squarespace, Wix) and custom-coded. Each has pros and cons, but you need to understand which you're getting and why.

Template-based sites are faster to build and cheaper, but they come with baggage: bloated code that loads slowly, security vulnerabilities from outdated plugins, and a design that's never truly unique. Custom-coded sites load faster, look better, and you own every line of code.

Ask these questions:

  • "Will my site be built on a template or coded from scratch?"
  • "What platform or CMS will my site run on?"
  • "Who will own the code and hosting after delivery?"
  • "How do I make simple updates after launch?"

3. Understand What SEO is Included

Many designers deliver a beautiful website with zero SEO consideration. The result: a site that looks great but never gets found. Basic on-page SEO should be included in every professional web design project - not sold as an expensive add-on.

At minimum, your website should include: properly structured H1/H2 headings, unique title tags and meta descriptions for every page, fast loading speed (a major Google ranking factor), mobile responsiveness, and clean URL structures. Ask your designer explicitly what SEO is included in the project scope and get the answer in writing.

4. Red Flags to Watch For

Experience has taught us the warning signs of a designer who will cause problems:

  • No written contract or scope of work. Without a document defining deliverables, timelines, and revision limits, disputes are inevitable.
  • Vague timelines. "A few weeks" is not a delivery date. Good designers give specific milestones.
  • No process for feedback and revisions. How many rounds of changes are included? What happens if you want significant changes mid-project?
  • They own the hosting. If your designer hosts your site on their account, they have leverage over you. Insist on hosting under your own account.
  • They can't explain why they make design decisions. Good designers make choices for reasons - user experience, conversion, readability. If they just say "it looks nice," that's not enough.

5. Match Budget to Realistic Expectations

Web design pricing ranges from €50 (Fiverr) to €50,000+ (large agencies). Here's a rough guide to what you get at each price point:

  • Under €200: Template installations with minimal customisation. Expect to do most of the work yourself.
  • €200–€800: Professional small-business websites with good design, basic SEO, and real customisation. This is where WebMaxPro operates.
  • €1,000–€5,000: Mid-market range. Can include custom features, e-commerce, and more pages.
  • €5,000+: Large agency pricing. Includes strategy, branding, UX research. Appropriate for larger businesses with complex needs.

The key insight: you don't need to spend €5,000 to get a genuinely great website. What you need is a designer who prioritises quality, communication, and results over charging as much as possible.

The Bottom Line

Choosing a web designer is an investment decision, not a commodity purchase. Take your time, ask the right questions, check real live work (not just screenshots), and make sure the contract is clear before signing anything. The best web designers make the process easy - because they've built systems that work.
