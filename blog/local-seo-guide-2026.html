Local SEO Guide for 2026

When someone searches for your service in your city, do you appear? Local SEO determines whether local customers find you or your competitors. Here's the complete 2026 playbook.

June 2026 · 9 min read · WebMaxPro Blog

What Is Local SEO and Why Does It Matter?

Local SEO is the practice of optimising your online presence to appear in search results when people search for businesses in a specific geographic area. When someone types "dentist Hasselt," "Italian restaurant Ghent," or "web designer Brussels" into Google, the results they see are heavily influenced by local SEO signals. The coveted "Local Pack" - the map with three business listings that appears above the organic results for local searches - captures a disproportionate share of clicks.

For any business that serves a specific geographic area - from a local café to a regional law firm - local SEO is the highest-ROI digital marketing activity available. It's free to implement (beyond your time), targets customers with clear purchase intent, and builds compounding visibility over time. Every month you invest in local SEO, your position improves and the returns compound.

The Google Business Profile: Your Local SEO Foundation

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the single most important local SEO asset you control. It's the listing that appears in Google Maps and the Local Pack, and it's completely free. If you haven't claimed yours at business.google.com, stop reading and do that first.

A fully optimised GBP includes:

  • Business name: Use your exact legal business name - don't stuff keywords into it, as this violates Google's guidelines and risks suspension.
  • Primary category: Choose the most specific category that accurately describes your main business. This is one of the most important ranking factors in the Local Pack.
  • Secondary categories: Add up to 9 additional categories for secondary services you offer.
  • Business description: A 750-character description of your business. Include your key services and location naturally, but write for humans first.
  • Photos: Upload at minimum: your logo, your exterior (so customers can find you), your interior, and photos of your products or services. Businesses with more photos rank better and receive more clicks.
  • Opening hours: Keep these meticulously up to date, including special hours for public holidays.
  • Services/Products: List every service or product you offer with descriptions. This gives Google more signals about your business's relevance for specific searches.
  • Q&A section: Pre-populate this with common questions and answers about your business. These appear publicly and can influence both rankings and conversion.

Google Reviews: The Local Pack's Most Powerful Ranking Factor

Reviews are simultaneously a ranking signal and a conversion signal. Businesses with more reviews, higher average ratings, and more recent reviews consistently outrank competitors with fewer or older reviews - even when other factors are similar. And for visitors who find you in the Local Pack, your star rating is one of the first things they see and heavily influences whether they click through.

The most effective way to accumulate reviews is to simply ask every satisfied customer directly. "Would you mind leaving us a Google review? It really helps us." Most people who have a good experience are happy to do it when asked - they just don't think to do it unprompted. Create a short URL that links directly to your review page (your GBP dashboard provides this) and share it via email, SMS, or WhatsApp after each successful interaction.

Always respond to reviews - both positive and negative. Responding to positive reviews signals engagement. Responding professionally and constructively to negative reviews demonstrates accountability and often turns a negative impression into a positive one for prospective customers reading the exchange.

On-Page Local SEO: Optimising Your Website for Local Searches

Your website needs to send clear local signals to complement your GBP. Key on-page local SEO elements:

  • Include your city in title tags: "Web Designer Antwerp | WebMaxPro" outperforms "Web Designer | WebMaxPro" for Antwerp searches.
  • Create a dedicated contact page with your full address: Include your street address, city, postal code, and country in plain text - not just in an image or embedded in a map.
  • Add LocalBusiness schema markup: This structured data tells Google your business name, address, phone, hours, and type in a machine-readable format. It's a significant local ranking signal.
  • Embed a Google Map: An embedded Google Map showing your location is a useful trust signal and a local SEO signal.
  • Create location-specific pages: If you serve multiple cities, create a dedicated page for each city with unique, locally relevant content rather than a single generic "service area" page.
  • Ensure NAP consistency: Your Name, Address, and Phone (NAP) must be identical on your website, Google Business Profile, and every directory where you're listed.

Local Citations and Directory Listings

A local citation is any online mention of your business's NAP (name, address, phone). Building citations across relevant directories signals to Google that your business is legitimate, established, and locally relevant. For Belgian businesses, prioritise: Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Facebook Business Page, Yelp, Foursquare, and industry-specific directories relevant to your sector.

The quality of citations matters more than quantity. Focus on authoritative directories - those that rank well in Google themselves and are widely used in your region. Avoid low-quality bulk citation submission services that list you on hundreds of spammy directories. And above all, ensure your NAP is 100% consistent across every listing - "Markt 12" and "Markt 12/A" are different to Google and inconsistent citations dilute your local SEO signal.

Local Link Building: Earning Links From Your Community

Links from other local websites - local newspapers, business associations, chambers of commerce, local blogs, event sponsors pages, partner businesses - send powerful local relevance signals to Google. These links are often easier to earn than national links because you have existing community relationships to leverage.

Practical local link building strategies: join your local Chamber of Commerce (they typically link to members on their website); sponsor local events, sports teams, or charities that maintain websites with sponsor pages; get featured in local press by doing something newsworthy; partner with complementary local businesses and link to each other; and submit your business to your city or municipality's official business directory if one exists. Each genuine local link strengthens both your local authority and your broader domain authority over time.
