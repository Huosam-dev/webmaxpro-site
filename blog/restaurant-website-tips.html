Restaurant Website Tips for 2026

A bad restaurant website loses you tables every single night. Here's exactly what your restaurant website needs to turn online visitors into paying guests.

June 2026 · 7 min read · WebMaxPro Blog

Why Your Restaurant Website Is Your Most Important Marketing Tool

When someone decides they want to try a new restaurant, the first thing they do is search Google. They find several options, open each website, and make a booking decision within about 30 seconds per site. In that 30 seconds, your website needs to do three things: make them hungry (with beautiful food photography), answer their key questions (menu, prices, location, hours), and make it dead easy to book. If it fails at any of these, they move on to the next result - even if your food is better.

The hospitality industry is intensely visual and intensely local. Your restaurant website needs to excel at both - looking stunning and ranking for the right local searches in your city. This guide covers exactly how to achieve both.

The Non-Negotiables: What Every Restaurant Website Must Have

  • A mobile-first design: The majority of restaurant searches happen on mobile, often right before a meal decision. Your site must be fast and beautiful on phones.
  • Professional food photography: Smartphone photos taken in dim restaurant lighting are not good enough. Invest in a professional food photographer - it's your most impactful marketing investment.
  • Your full menu with prices: Never make visitors click away to a PDF menu. Display your menu directly on the page, properly formatted and easy to read on mobile.
  • Booking functionality: An online reservation form or integration with a booking system (TheFork, OpenTable, Resy) directly on your website. Never just list a phone number as the only booking method.
  • Allergen information: Legally required in the EU and increasingly expected by diners. Include allergen details for every dish or a clear statement about your allergen policy.
  • Your exact address with a map embed: Not just the street address - include a Google Maps embed so visitors can get directions with one tap.
  • Opening hours: Displayed prominently, including any special holiday hours. Nothing is more frustrating than arriving at a restaurant that's closed.

Food Photography: The Make-or-Break Factor

We've mentioned it once but it deserves its own section. Restaurant websites live or die on photography. The difference between a restaurant website with professional food photography and one with mediocre photos is visible within a fraction of a second - and it directly affects booking rates. Professional food photography makes food look genuinely appetising: the right lighting, the right angle, the right styling, the right moment of capture.

For a restaurant investing in a new website, budget for at least a half-day professional food photography session. Shoot your 8-12 most popular and most photogenic dishes, plus several ambience shots of the space during service, and one or two shots of your team. These images will serve you for 2-3 years across your website, social media, and print materials. The ROI is exceptional.

How to Rank on Google for Local Restaurant Searches

The most valuable Google searches for your restaurant are things like "Italian restaurant Antwerp," "best sushi Brussels," "restaurant open Sunday Ghent centrum." To rank for these, you need two things working together: a well-optimised website and a strong Google Business Profile.

For your website: include your city and cuisine type in your page titles, H1 heading, and throughout your content naturally. Add structured data (schema markup) for restaurants - this tells Google explicitly your cuisine type, price range, opening hours, and address. Make sure your address on your website matches exactly what's on your Google Business Profile. Build your site to load fast on mobile.

For your Google Business Profile: ensure your category is correctly set ("Italian Restaurant," "Sushi Restaurant," etc.), upload dozens of photos, respond to every review, post regular updates, and enable the booking link feature to allow direct reservations from your Google listing.

Online Menus: Display, Don't Link

One of the most common restaurant website mistakes is linking to a PDF menu rather than displaying the menu directly on the page. PDF menus are bad for almost every reason: they're not mobile-friendly, they can't be indexed by Google, they require a separate app to open on some phones, and they can't be updated without uploading a new file. Display your menu as actual HTML text on your website. This makes it readable on any device, searchable by Google (ranking opportunity!), and easy to update yourself.

Use clear sections for each course, readable font sizes, and consider adding photos for your signature dishes. A well-designed digital menu on your website is also a sales tool - good menu design, with strategic placement of high-margin items and compelling descriptions, can influence what guests order before they even arrive.

Manage Your Online Reputation Through Your Website

Your website should actively build your restaurant's reputation, not just display information. Include a reviews section that showcases your best Google and TripAdvisor reviews directly on your site - this builds trust for visitors who find you through search rather than review platforms. Feature press coverage, food blog features, and awards prominently. If a food critic reviewed you favourably in a local publication, that quote belongs on your homepage.

Also ensure your website makes it easy for happy guests to leave reviews. A simple "Enjoyed your visit? Leave us a review" footer link to your Google Business Profile costs nothing to implement and can significantly increase the volume of positive reviews your restaurant accumulates over time.
