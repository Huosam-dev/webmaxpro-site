How Much Does a Website Cost in 2026?

Pricing in the web design industry is notoriously opaque. This guide breaks down exactly what you'll pay and what you should expect to get at every price point.

June 2026 · 8 min read · WebMaxPro Blog

The Short Answer: It Depends on Four Things

Website cost in 2026 varies enormously - from under €100 for a DIY template to €100,000+ for a complex e-commerce platform built by a large agency. The price is determined by four main factors:

  • Who builds it: DIY, freelancer, boutique studio, or large agency
  • How it's built: Template/page builder vs. custom-coded
  • What's included: Number of pages, features, copywriting, SEO
  • Ongoing costs: Hosting, maintenance, software licences

Most small and medium businesses need to spend between €200 and €2,000 for a genuinely effective professional website. Let's break down each tier.

What's Often Not Included in the Price

Many web design quotes look attractive until you see the extras. Be sure to ask what's included before signing. Common add-ons that raise the final price include:

  • Copywriting: Writing the actual text for your website. Often not included.
  • Photography: Stock photos or professional photography for your site.
  • SEO setup: Many designers deliver sites with zero SEO work done.
  • Hosting setup: Getting the site live on a server.
  • Domain registration: Buying and connecting your domain name.
  • Revisions beyond the first round: Extra revision rounds often cost extra.
  • Maintenance after launch: Updates, security patches, and content changes.

Always ask for an itemised quote that lists exactly what's included so there are no surprises.

Ongoing Annual Costs to Budget For

Your website isn't a one-off expense - it has recurring costs every year. Here's what to budget:

  • Domain name: €10–€30/year depending on the extension (.com, .be, .co.uk, etc.)
  • Web hosting: €50–€300/year for quality shared or cloud hosting
  • SSL certificate: Often included free with hosting (Let's Encrypt)
  • Maintenance: If on WordPress, budget €100–€500/year for updates and security
  • Content updates: If you need regular updates, budget for developer time or a CMS

A custom-coded static website has the lowest ongoing costs - just hosting and your domain. WordPress sites have higher maintenance overhead due to plugin updates and security risks.

Is Cheap Web Design a False Economy?

Yes and no. A €50 Fiverr website will almost certainly underperform and may need to be rebuilt within 12 months - making it more expensive in the long run. But there's a sweet spot between "dirt cheap" and "agency rates" where you can get genuinely excellent work.

The key is finding a designer who delivers real quality at a fair price - not someone who uses a template and charges for custom work, and not an agency with 15 staff members whose overhead inflates every invoice. How to Get the Best Value in 2026

To get maximum value from your web design investment:

  • Prepare your content (text, photos, logo) before engaging a designer - this saves significant time and cost
  • Be specific about what you need: number of pages, features, and goals
  • Ask for a fixed-price quote, not hourly rates
  • Check real live portfolio work, not just screenshots
  • Prioritise speed and SEO in the brief - these have the highest ROI
  • Don't overbuild: a well-designed 5-page site outperforms a mediocre 20-page site
